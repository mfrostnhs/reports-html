Executive summary

Progress is visible, but disabled people continue to experience significant inequalities in opportunity, people processes and day-to-day experience.

This report sets out Cambridge University Hospitals' performance against the 2025/26 Workforce Disability Equality Standard and the proposed action plan for 2026/27.

Disability declaration on ESR increased for the third consecutive year to 6.93%. This remains substantially below the 22.28% identifying as disabled through the National Staff Survey, with declaration particularly low among Medical and Dental staff. Disabled representation remains limited at senior levels and is slightly lower on the Board than across the wider workforce.

The most significant people-process concern is formal capability. Disabled people were 3.03 times more likely than non-disabled people to enter formal capability, compared with 2.13 times in the previous year. Confidence in equal opportunities for career progression also deteriorated, with 49% of disabled people reporting equal opportunities compared with 57% of non-disabled people.

Everyday experience remains less positive. The greatest concern is abuse from patients, relatives and the public, reported by 31% of disabled people compared with 22% of non-disabled people. Disabled people are also more likely to feel pressure to work when unwell and less likely to feel valued by CUH.

There are important areas of progress. 80% of disabled people report that adequate workplace adjustments have been made, exceeding the NHS benchmark. Reporting of harassment and bullying is close to parity, and disabled people continue to report higher overall engagement than non-disabled people, although engagement reduced slightly for both groups in 2025.

The 2026/27 action plan focuses on disability declaration, equitable career and development pathways, safeguards in capability processes, practical support for managers, Behaviours Matter, responses to unacceptable behaviour, co-production with staff networks, and Executive Sponsors for the Purple and Open Minds Staff Networks.

Delivery will be reviewed quarterly by Workforce SMT, with formal assurance to the People, Education and Culture Committee three times a year.

Understanding the Workforce Disability Equality Standard

The WDES is a national framework used to understand whether disabled and non-disabled people experience CUH differently. It brings together workforce data and Staff Survey results covering representation, opportunity, experience and inclusion.

1. Representation and access

Who is represented in the workforce, who gets access to jobs, and who is visible in governance.

  • Workforce representation
  • Appointment from shortlisting
  • Board representation

2. Fair people processes and opportunity

Whether people processes and development opportunities are experienced equitably.

  • Formal capability processes
  • Equal opportunities to progress
  • Pressure to work when unwell

3. Everyday experience at CUH

What disabled people experience day to day, including safety, respect and practical support.

  • Harassment, bullying and abuse
  • Feeling valued
  • Reasonable adjustments

4. Engagement and voice

How connected disabled people feel to CUH, and how lived experience shapes improvement.

  • Staff engagement
  • The voice of disabled people

WDES 2026/27 action plan

Governance, accountability and visibility: Delivery will be reviewed quarterly by Workforce SMT, with formal assurance to the People, Education and Culture Committee three times a year. Priorities will be embedded into EDI Strategy delivery plans, with progress, outcomes and slippage reported.

Representation and access

Disability declaration

Run targeted communications and engagement briefings through inductions and in areas with low declaration rates, such as Medical and Dental staffing.

Owner: Head of Workforce EDI
Milestone: Targeted activity launched by Q1 2027/28

Visible career and development pathways

Make career pathways, progression routes, training and development opportunities clear, accessible and easy to find. Use workforce data to identify gaps and target mentoring, sponsorship and development support for disabled people, particularly those seeking senior and leadership roles.

Owner: Director of CLL
Milestone: Pathways published and targeted support launched by Q4 2026/27

Fair people processes and opportunity

Fair and proportionate people processes

Introduce a model of pre-action review for capability cases involving disabled staff.

Owner: Director of Workforce
Milestone: Pre-action review implemented by Q1 2027/28

Manager capability and early support

Provide targeted training and practical support for managers on disability, reasonable adjustments, disability leave, attendance management and early resolution. Make guidance and tools easy to find and provide advice on complex cases.

Owner: Director of CLL
Milestone: Training and central guidance available by end of Q4 2026/27

Everyday experience at CUH

Reduction in bullying, harassment and abuse from staff

Launch and embed the CUH behavioural framework, Behaviours Matter, supporting a zero-tolerance response to racism, bullying, harassment, abuse and discrimination.

Owner: Director of CLL
Milestone: Trust-wide launch during Q3 2026/27

Reduction in bullying, harassment and abuse from patients and the public

Develop a coordinated Trust-wide approach across Quality and Safety, Workforce, Estates and Security to prevent and respond to unacceptable behaviour. Set clear expectations, strengthen reporting and escalation, support affected people and use learning to inform future action.

Owner: To be confirmed
Milestone: Executive ownership and Trust-wide approach agreed by Q4 2026/27

Engagement and staff voice

Engagement and co-production

Involve Staff Networks, Freedom to Speak Up, staff-side colleagues and key stakeholders in shaping, delivering and monitoring improvement activity.

Owner: Head of Workforce EDI
Milestone: Delivery plans agreed by end of Q3 2026/27, with quarterly review thereafter

Executive sponsorship and lived experience

Appoint Executive Sponsors for the Purple and Open Minds Staff Networks, with clear expectations to engage regularly, champion lived experience and report actions taken in response.

Owner: Head of Workforce EDI
Milestone: Sponsors appointed and initial engagement completed by end of Q3 2026/27

Conclusion and next steps

Conclusion

  • CUH continues to make progress in disability inclusion, particularly in disability declaration, workplace adjustments, confidence in reporting concerns and engagement among disabled staff.
  • Important inequalities remain in experiences of harassment, bullying and abuse, career progression, feeling valued and pressure to work when unwell.
  • The most significant workforce process concern is the disproportionate likelihood of disabled staff entering formal capability processes. The next phase must focus on inclusive people processes, leadership practice and culture, not only individual support.

Next steps

  • Co-produce the detailed 2026/27 delivery plan with the Purple Network, Open Minds Network, staff-side colleagues, senior leaders and relevant teams. Give every action a named owner, milestone, measure of success and reporting route.
  • Monitor delivery through workforce governance, including Workforce Compliance and Workforce SMT, with progress reported through to the People, Education and Culture Committee.
  • Review progress against WDES measures and wider workforce intelligence to understand whether actions create meaningful improvements in experience, opportunity and outcomes.
We are making progress, but persistent inequalities remain. The next phase is about translating agreed actions into a co-produced, accountable delivery plan.

Metric 8: Adequate workplace adjustments

Percentage of disabled staff saying adequate adjustments were made to enable them to work.

What the data shows

  • Disabled staff increasingly report that adequate adjustments have been made, rising from approximately 71% in 2022 to approximately 80% in 2025.
